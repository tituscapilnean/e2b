2 Why this is the best way

Vasek's posts get almost twice the engagement of E2B's brand account, yet most of what he publishes is announcements and reactions to other people's takes. His best idea, laid out in his essay Sandboxes are dead, rarely gets a post of its own in his feed, so I saw an opportunity to give it more reach.

The goal: top of the funnel with enterprise buyers

I chose a thought-leadership angle aimed at enterprise buyers on purpose. The job of these posts is to reach CTOs, Heads of AI, and platform leads at AI labs, fast-scaling agent companies, and large enterprises, and get them thinking about how they'll run agents in production. Purpose-built sandboxes, and E2B, should be part of that thinking long before anyone writes an RFP.

That's top-of-funnel work, so the posts are written to travel organically and to be boosted to the exact accounts we want. More on that below.

Adapting the style to the persona

I kept what makes the voice Vasek's: real numbers up front, plain words, "we" more than "I," almost no emoji or hashtags. Then I adapted the style to the persona these posts need to reach.

His most viral posts are lowercase one-liners like "startups should not be building coffee shops" (126k views). They work for founders and engineers on X, and they're the wrong register for a CTO deciding where thousands of agents will run. The posts I proposed aim to show up with Vasek's unique perspective in front of potential buyers, not just meme consumers.

Why this angle

Any agent-infrastructure company can say "agents need real computers." Only E2B can say it has run 2 billion sandboxes and learned what an agent will need before it starts. Leading with that claim moves the conversation away from boot time and price per vCPU-hour. It gives buyers a new question that E2B wins ("how many agents like yours have you run?") and backs The AI Agent Cloud category with proof. Genspark's CTO handles build vs. buy in his own words.

Vasek's account already gets about 1.7 to 1.8x the brand's median engagement, so the posts start there. LinkedIn Thought Leader Ads then let us put budget behind his post and target it by title, company size, or account list. That's why the copy has no link, no launch language, and no milestone that goes stale. LinkedIn carries the full argument for buyers, and X carries the same idea in a few lines for engineers at the labs. Organic tests the message, and paid scales the winner into the accounts that matter.

How I made it

I ran this as an agentic workflow:

  • Claude Code and Apify to pull and analyze 411 of Vasek's and E2B's posts, plus his long-form writing
  • Claude for drafts, tied to a fact list with a source for every number
  • a second AI agent as an independent reviewer, until it had nothing left to flag
  • me as the editor: the angle, the audience, and the final edits are mine

How I'd measure it

Engagement from target titles and accounts, cost per engaged buyer on the boosted version, and pipeline influenced by those accounts within 60 to 90 days.
